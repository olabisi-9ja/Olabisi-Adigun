S.A.F.E.

A campus-wide emergency reporting platform that helps students send alerts faster and helps security teams respond with better context.

RoleLead Engineer
PlatformMobile and web
FocusSafety workflows
StackReact Native, Node.js, Supabase

Problem

Students often need to report incidents under pressure, but traditional reporting channels add friction at the worst possible time. Calls can be missed, messages can lack location context, and response teams may not know how urgent a report is until they arrive.

S.A.F.E. was designed around a practical question: how quickly can a student create a useful, actionable report when they are stressed?

My Role

I led the product and engineering work across the mobile reporting flow, backend incident intake, and the security dashboard. The goal was to keep the student experience calm while giving responders the information they need to act.

Product Direction

Design principle

The interface should never feel like paperwork. In an emergency, the product must reduce decisions, collect only what matters, and confirm that the report moved forward.

The student flow prioritizes fast reporting, clear status feedback, and location context. The dashboard prioritizes triage, assignment, and visibility across active reports.

System Architecture

  • Mobile app: incident reporting, status updates, and device location capture.
  • API layer: report ingestion, authentication, storage, and notification triggers.
  • Security dashboard: live queue management for reports, responders, and resolution state.
  • Offline support: local persistence for reports until the device can send data reliably.

Delivery

The project required careful tradeoffs between speed, privacy, and reliability. I focused on keeping the first report action short, keeping status language clear, and making sure the responder view exposed the right data without visual noise.

Outcome

S.A.F.E. became a stronger blueprint for institutional safety tools: simple enough for students, structured enough for security teams, and technical enough to work in imperfect connectivity.
